1. Thermodynamic Units

Boiler

boiler_simulink.png

The boiler is the primary heat source in the Rankine cycle. It receives high-pressure subcooled liquid from the feedwater pump and adds heat generated by combustion to convert the water into high-pressure superheated steam.

Thermodynamic Process: Isobaric Heat Addition (Constant Pressure). The process raises the enthalpy of the working fluid significantly to store the energy needed to drive the turbine.

Energy Balance Equations:

Applying the First Law of Thermodynamics for a steady-flow control volume:

$$ q_{in} = h_{out} - h_{in} $$

Where hout is the enthalpy of the exiting steam, and hin is the enthalpy of the entering water.

The total heat transfer rate is calculated as:

$$ \dot{Q}_{in} = \dot{m}_{steam} (h_{out} - h_{in}) $$

Simulink Context: In the Simscape Two-Phase Fluid library, this block models a heated pipe or volume where boundary heat flux is applied to the mass flow entering from Conn1, outputting energized steam at Conn2.

Steam Turbine

turbine_simulink.png

The steam turbine is the prime mover of the cycle. It converts the thermal energy of the high-pressure steam into mechanical rotational energy by allowing the steam to expand through its blades.

Thermodynamic Process: Isentropic Expansion. As steam expands from boiler pressure to condenser pressure, its internal energy decreases, and this energy is captured as mechanical work.

Power Equations:

$$ w_{t} = h_{in} - h_{out} $$

The total power produced depends on the mass flow rate and the enthalpy drop across the turbine:

$$ P_{mech} = \dot{m} \times (h_{in} - h_{out}) \times \eta_{t} $$

Simulink Context: In your model, the Turbine block acts as a bridge. It converts fluid power into mechanical power, which is then transmitted via the green mechanical shaft connection to the Synchronous Generator.

Condenser

Condenser_simulink.png

The condenser acts as a heat sink, where the low-pressure steam exiting the turbine is cooled down to become saturated liquid. This phase change is crucial to allow the pump to recirculate the water back to the boiler.

Thermodynamic Process: Isobaric Heat Rejection. The latent heat of vaporization is removed at a constant pressure, turning the steam-water mixture into 100% liquid.

Heat Transfer Equations:

$$ q_{out} = h_{in} - h_{out} $$

The energy removed by the cooling medium (water or air) is defined as:

$$ \dot{Q}_{out} = \dot{m} \times (h_{in} - h_{out}) $$

Simulink Context: This block simulates the heat transfer between the working fluid and an external cooling environment. It ensures the fluid reaching the Pump is in a liquid state to prevent cavitation and ensure cycle stability.

Feedwater Pump

pump_simulink.png

The feedwater pump is the mechanical heart that drives the cycle. It consumes a small amount of work to pressurize the saturated liquid water exiting the condenser, pushing it into the high-pressure environment of the boiler.

Thermodynamic Process: Isentropic Compression. Because liquids are nearly incompressible, the work required to raise the pressure is a tiny fraction of the work produced by the turbine, making the Rankine cycle highly efficient.

Work Equations:

The specific work consumed can be calculated using enthalpy or specific volume ($v$) and pressure limits:

$$ w_{p} = h_{out} - h_{in} \approx v(P_{boiler} - P_{condenser}) $$

The total power consumed by the pump is:

$$ \dot{W}_{p} = \dot{m} \times w_{p} $$

Simulink Context: The Pump block in the Simscape Two-Phase Fluid library applies mechanical energy to the fluid flow, simulating the pressure boost required to overcome the boiler's pressure. It connects the low-pressure side (Conn2) back to the high-pressure side (Conn1).

2. Electrical Systems

Synchronous Generator

Generator_simulink.png

The Synchronous Generator converts the mechanical rotational energy produced by the steam turbine into three-phase electrical AC power using the principle of electromagnetic induction.

Operating Principle: The turbine drives the generator's rotor. A magnetic field on the rotor (controlled by the AVR) induces an alternating voltage in the stator windings as it spins, creating the electrical power output.

Power Equations:

The mechanical power entering the generator is the product of torque and angular velocity:

$$ P_{mech} = \tau \times \omega $$

The three-phase electrical active power generated is:

$$ P_{elec} = \sqrt{3} \cdot V_L \cdot I_L \cdot \cos(\phi) $$

Simulink Context: In the model, the Generator block receives mechanical shaft speed and torque from the Turbine (via the 'mec' port). It outputs 3-phase voltage and current (Vabc, Iabc) to the electrical load while being excited by the 'E' signal from the AVR.

Mechanical Load / Grid

grid_simulink.png

The Mechanical Load or Grid represents the power consumers (cities, factories) or the national grid connected to the power plant. It dictates how much current is drawn from the generator based on the energy demand.

Operating Principle: The load consumes Active Power (for actual work) and Reactive Power (for magnetic fields). A sudden change in the load directly impacts the generator's voltage and the turbine's speed, triggering the control systems (AVR and Governor) to respond.

Power Equations:

The power drawn by a three-phase load is calculated as:

$$ P = \sqrt{3} \cdot V_L \cdot I_L \cdot \cos(\phi) $$
$$ Q = \sqrt{3} \cdot V_L \cdot I_L \cdot \sin(\phi) $$

Simulink Context: The Mechanical Load block is connected to the generator's output terminals. It acts as a sink for the electrical power. By varying the parameters of this block during simulation, you can observe how the plant handles sudden load rejections or additions.

3. Control & Measurement

Speed Controller (Governor)

Governor_simulink.png

The Speed Controller (Governor) acts as the brain for mechanical stability. It maintains the turbine's rotational speed, which strictly dictates the electrical grid frequency (e.g., 50 Hz or 60 Hz).

Control Principle: It compares the actual rotor speed with a reference setpoint. If the load increases and speed drops, the controller commands the steam valve to open wider, admitting more steam to match the demand.

Control Logic:

Using a PID or PI control mechanism, it calculates the error:

$$ e(t) = \omega_{ref} - \omega_{actual} $$

Simulink Context: The 'Speed Controller' block reads the measured speed and outputs a dynamic control signal to the Turbine block. This signal acts as a valve position command (from 0 to 1) to regulate the steam mass flow rate.

AVR

avr_simulink.png

The Automatic Voltage Regulator (AVR) ensures electrical stability by maintaining a constant terminal voltage at the generator's output under varying load conditions.

Control Principle: As electrical demand fluctuates, the terminal voltage can drop or spike. The AVR senses these changes and continuously adjusts the DC excitation current supplied to the generator's rotor electromagnet.

Control Logic:

The AVR adjusts the field voltage based on the difference between the desired voltage and the measured voltage:

$$ \Delta V_{f} = K \cdot (V_{ref} - V_{actual}) $$

Simulink Context: The AVR block monitors the terminal voltage (Va). If a voltage drop is detected, it increases the excitation voltage sent to the 'E' port of the Generator, strengthening the magnetic field to restore the voltage to its nominal value.

Measurement Scopes

Scopes_simulink.png

The Measurement Scopes represent the monitoring instruments found in a power plant's control room. They are the analytical tools used by engineers to observe the real-time dynamic behavior and stability of the system.

Operating Principle: These tools track critical variables over time, such as rotor speed, terminal voltage, and output current. They are essential for analyzing the "Transient Response" (how the system reacts to sudden disturbances) and the "Steady-State" performance.

Monitored Parameters:

  • Vabc & Iabc: Monitors the three-phase sinusoidal waveforms to ensure voltage stability and proper synchronization.
  • Rotor Speed ($\omega_m$): Tracks the mechanical RPM to ensure the grid frequency remains constant at nominal values.
  • Electrical Power ($P_e$): Measures the instantaneous active power delivered to the load.

Simulink Context: The Scope blocks collect data arrays from the system's sensors (like the 'Measurement Power' and Generator outputs) and plot them dynamically against simulation time, providing a visual proof of the control systems' effectiveness.
